# OctoAcme Project Management Overview

OctoAcme manages work through a clear, repeatable lifecycle: initiation, planning, execution, release, and retrospective. Projects begin with a concise One-pager that defines the problem, SMART objectives, success metrics, stakeholders, and a high-level timeline. A lightweight Decision Gate ensures initiatives proceed to planning only when success metrics, stakeholder alignment, and team availability are confirmed. Planning produces a prioritized backlog, a Definition of Done, a release plan with milestones, and a simple risk register to capture and track known risks.

Execution follows an explicit board workflow (Backlog → Ready → In Progress → In Review → QA → Done) and a disciplined pull request process that favors small, reviewable changes linked to issues with clear acceptance criteria. Sprint and iteration planning are timeboxed and focused on items that meet the Definition of Done, while estimations use T-shirt sizing or story points depending on team preference. Cross-team dependencies and blockers are tracked on the project board and escalated through the team cadence to ensure timely resolution.

Roles are clearly defined: Product Managers own outcomes and success metrics; Project Managers coordinate delivery, risks, and communication; Developers implement and test changes; QA validates acceptance criteria; and stakeholders provide inputs and approvals. Communication is driven by a predictable cadence—daily standups, weekly delivery syncs, weekly PM+PdM alignment, and monthly stakeholder updates—supplemented by templates for weekly status and incident communications so messages remain concise and consistent.

Quality assurance and release practices are enforced end-to-end. New work includes unit and integration tests, with smoke tests for critical flows and security scans in CI. Releases follow pre-release checks (passing CI, acceptance criteria met, release notes, and rollback plans) and a deployment checklist for staging and production. Retrospectives after sprints, releases, and incidents capture lessons and create prioritized action items that are tracked back into the backlog to drive continuous improvement.
